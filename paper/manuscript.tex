\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Battery-electric heavy-duty tractors are discussed for Moroccan long-distance logistics, including temperature-controlled export chains from Souss-Massa toward northern gateways. Infrastructure planners must decide \emph{where} en-route charging might be needed and \emph{whether} remaining routes are energy-feasible under cargo, terrain, and power limits. Commercial and academic models increasingly merge location and routing \cite{VidottePlaza2026TRC}, yet field data on refrigerated electric truck operations on Moroccan motorways remain scarce.

This paper asks a planning question: \textbf{how much operational freight service can joint charger placement and energy-aware multi-route planning unlock} on a Moroccan refrigerated corridor when missions must obey physics, speed policy, and professional-driver pause rules? Our flagship direction is \textbf{northbound} Agadir$\rightarrow$Casablanca with an illustrative firm-ripe tomato duty cycle; southbound legs are research return-flow scenarios unless observed OD counts are supplied.

\paragraph{Non-claims.} We do not assert a new joint location--routing formulation, a new SOC label-setting algorithm, or continuous global optimality of any adaptive discretization rule. We do not recommend specific ADM investments, and we do not treat equal mission weights as observed freight volumes.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Operational coverage vs infrastructure budget} on a directed Moroccan corridor, requiring continuous physics replay, action audit, and d\'ecret~2-10-314 schedule validity --- not energy feasibility alone.
  \item Mechanism-separated evidence that \textbf{route flexibility}, \textbf{charger location}, and \textbf{driver-rest timing} each change which missions are executable; joint multi-route siting and greedy siting \emph{tie} at full operational coverage with two Benguerir directed sites.
  \item A payload$\times$SOC and six-OD depot evidence panel separating charger-budget gains from route-flexibility gains, with chronological HOS pads (including independent rest at verified on-path ADM nodes) kept as \textbf{validation} of operational coverage rather than the main storyline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Literature review}
\label{sec:lit}

\subsection{Charger location with electric truck routing}

Vidotte Plaza et al.\ \cite{VidottePlaza2026TRC} formulate the charger location problem with routing and driver working hours for long-haul electric heavy-duty trucks, using time-expanded network modeling. That work sets the benchmark: joint siting and routing is established prior art. Our study complements it by stress-testing \textbf{finite energy discretization} on a DEM-refrigerated corridor instance rather than proposing a competing joint model.

\subsection{Refrigerated electrified freight energy}

Bank et al.\ \cite{Bank2025IJR} provide physics-oriented refrigerated transport energy modeling with field validation. We cite their work as the thermal realism bar but use transparent UA-based auxiliary scenarios without claiming calibration to Moroccan transport réfrigéré units (TRUs).

\subsection{Resource-constrained shortest paths and labeling}

Multi-resource label-setting for electric vehicle routing is a mature family. Our MRLS implementation is an engineering exact solver on a bounded decision graph with optional computational limits; \textbf{novelty of the label algorithm itself is not claimed}.

\subsection{Gap addressed here}

The gap we address is \textbf{useful joint routing and infrastructure planning under realistic Moroccan cold-chain operating constraints}: refrigeration, DEM physics, schedule-valid driver rests, and schedule-aware site selection --- with discretization diagnostics retained as reliability validation rather than the headline contribution.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Infrastructure layer}

Let $Y$ denote binary installation variables for candidate sites $s\in\mathcal{S}$ on the studied carriageway. A budget $B$ caps $\sum_s y_s$. Candidates on our northbound pilot include \textbf{hypothetical} truck chargers at Benguerir and Settat topology nodes (notation $y_{\mathrm{benguerir\_ac}}$, $y_{\mathrm{settat\_ac}}$). Operating ADM car chargers do not imply HGV 350~kW availability.

The \textbf{primary} objective is \emph{operational} coverage of an equal-weight three-mission research portfolio (Casablanca$\rightarrow$Marrakech, Casablanca$\rightarrow$Agadir, Agadir$\rightarrow$Casablanca). A lexicographic secondary criterion prefers fewer sites then lower en-route incremental cost among operationally served missions. Costs remain normalized research units, not Moroccan investment optima.

\subsection{Routing layer}

For each mission $m$, a path-union decision graph $\mathcal{G}_m$ encodes up to $K$ OSM-verified alternatives (canonical $K{=}4$ on the northbound Agadir$\rightarrow$Casablanca study). Arc actions include DRIVE, CHARGE at open stations, and WAIT. State resources include clock time, battery energy on a finite grid, and optionally route-choice labels. Multi-route optima are compared to fixed-shortest baselines using \textbf{same-graph nesting} via arc masks.

\subsection{Reliability statuses}

We distinguish:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{MODEL\_ONLY}: finite discrete certificate;
  \item \textbf{SIMULATION\_REPLAY\_VALIDATED}: discrete solution replayed continuously without trace break on committed geometry/DEM;
  \item \textbf{UNRESOLVED}: grid audit pending, limit reached, or missing CSV regeneration.
\end{itemize}
Field \textbf{PHYSICALLY\_VERIFIED} is intentionally \textbf{not} used.

\section{Energy and refrigeration model}
\label{sec:energy}

Traction demand combines mass, gradient macros sampled from COP-DEM GLO-30 \cite{CopernicusDEMGLO30}, and a peak-power-aware speed policy. Battery energy evolves in steps of quantum $q$ (kWh) with \textbf{conservative flooring} after DRIVE table construction and stationary snap after CHARGE/WAIT to avoid optimistic feasibility.

Refrigerated auxiliary load follows YAML scenarios (firm-ripe tomato proxy) inspired by cold-chain physics literature \cite{Bank2025IJR} but \textbf{not} calibrated to operator logs. Ablation ladders (nominal / high thermal conductance) bound --- not eliminate --- thermal uncertainty.

Charging uses hypothetical fixed station power (e.g.\ 350~kW planning class) with vehicle-side acceptance caps from manufacturer YAMLs \cite{MercedesBenzEActros600,VolvoFHElectric}.

\section{Moroccan network, DEM, and candidate sites}
\label{sec:morocco}

\subsection{Corridor and freight direction}

The directed pilot follows the Agadir--Marrakech--Casablanca motorway extract \cite{OpenStreetMapMoroccoMotorway}. External sources motivate northbound agricultural exports; the optimization graph ends at Casablanca (no Tanger Med expansion in this freeze).

\subsection{DEM and speeds}

Elevation macros are precomputed along directed arcs. Research cruise defaults use 80~km/h on motorways (aligned with d\'ecret~2-10-420 art.~18 maxima for HGV $>19$~t). A 40~km/h adaptive-power floor rejects walking-speed crawl; it is not a legal normal-operation minimum (legal normal circulation minimum is 60~km/h). Successful portfolio traces show minimum contracted-arc means of 60~km/h.

\subsection{ADM sites}

Service-area identities follow ADM publications \cite{ADMMoroccoServiceAreas}. Attachment to the directed graph respects carriageway direction audits; mis-attachment across reverse rows is a documented failure mode corrected in repository scripts. \textbf{All chargers in the northbound flagship are hypothetical HGV posts collocated with ADM topology, not verified operating assets.}

On the flagship northbound eActros tomato run ($q\in\{5,2,1\}$), continuous replay yields \textbf{SIMULATION\_REPLAY\_VALIDATED} traces of length \textbf{429.15~km} (COMPLETE). \section{Solution approach: MRLS and branch-and-bound}
\label{sec:solvers}

\subsection{Multi-resource label setting (MRLS)}

The lower-level problem is solved by exact multi-resource label setting on $\mathcal{G}_m$ with Pareto pruning and optional limits. This is standard engineering practice; \textbf{we claim no algorithmic novelty for SOC labeling}.

\subsection{Placement via enumeration and B\&B}

The upper level evaluates mission costs for each feasible $y$-pattern. Exhaustive enumeration certifies small Moroccan budgets; branch-and-bound with coverage upper bounds and budget-feasible incumbents accelerates some --- not all --- synthetic instances. On the four-site corridor portfolio we report \textbf{exhaustive enumeration} as the joint optimum certificate; branch-and-bound is available in the repository but is not claimed as a runtime advantage on this instance.

A certification fix decouples \emph{routing resolved} from \emph{budget feasible} when pruning supersets, preventing false elimination of optimal leaves (repository note \texttt{reports/bb\_relaxed\_bound\_certification.md}).

\subsection{Continuous replay}

Selected discrete solutions undergo continuous replay with the same arc geometry, DEM macros, and efficiency assumptions. Passing replay yields \textbf{SIMULATION\_REPLAY\_VALIDATED}; it does not prove uniqueness of continuous-feasible routes.

\section{Discretization reliability and energy-grid convergence}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Motivation}

Infrastructure studies often inherit a fixed SOC quantum $q$. If the same physical mission is INFEASIBLE at $q{=}10$ but OPTIMAL at $q\in\{5,2,1\}$ on an identical graph, a planner might falsely conclude that additional sites or different vehicles are mandatory. We treat this as a \textbf{numerical instability symptom}, not physical law.

\subsection{Northbound canonical four-alternative panel}

On the eActros northbound tomato scenario with peak-adaptive speeds:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Coarse grids can reject missions that finer grids restore on the same graph (e.g.\ reserve-SOC 15\%/20\% INFEASIBLE at $q{=}5$ but OPTIMAL at $q{=}2$ with Benguerir).
  \item $q\in\{5,2,1\}$ northbound tomato panel: OPTIMAL, \textbf{SIMULATION\_REPLAY\_VALIDATED}, install $\{y_{\mathrm{benguerir\_ac}}\}$ only, identical 25-DRIVE sequence and \texttt{audit\_ok=True} (Table~\ref{tab:action-audit}).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Interpretation guardrail:} installation stability on this panel does \textbf{not} imply Benguerir is universally optimal across vehicles, SOC, payload, or southbound portfolios.

\subsection{Action-exact rounding audit (northbound flagship)}

Table~\ref{tab:action-audit} summarizes \texttt{results/final\_action\_rounding\_audit.csv}. Discrete grid charge totals \textbf{change with} $q$ (175/110/94~kWh) and therefore do \textbf{not} represent converged continuous charging need; they track conservative flooring of per-action energy increments. \subsection{Conservative flooring policy}

Flooring is retained because removing it risks optimistic discrete charging gains (unit tests document zero discrete gain cases). We do not market flooring as a new continuous optimal control policy.

\section{Baseline ladder and comparative evaluation}
\label{sec:baselines}

Baselines nest progressively richer decisions under identical vehicles, demand, SOC, payload, physics, stopping policy, and deadlines:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[A.] No new infrastructure;
  \item[B.] Fixed shortest routes + conventional sites;
  \item[C.] Fixed shortest routes + operationally optimized site placement;
  \item[D.] Multi-route MRLS + the same conventional sites;
  \item[E.] Joint site placement + multi-route MRLS via \textbf{exhaustive enumeration} (four sites);
  \item[F.] Independent sequential greedy on operational coverage (early-stop when coverage saturates).
\end{enumerate}
Fairness forbids mixing speed policies or quantization regimes across methods. On the corrected eActros portfolio, operational coverage at budget~B2 is $1.00$ for E and F (tie; both install $\{y_{\mathrm{benguerir\_ac}},y_{\mathrm{benguerir\_ca}}\}$), versus $0.67$ for C and $0.33$ for D (\texttt{results/final\_operational\_coverage\_vs\_budget.csv}). We do not claim that enumeration beats greedy.

\subsection{DEM, refrigeration, and charger power}

Firm-ripe tomato TRU power under UA$=80$~W/K is modest at cool ambients ($\approx 0.37$~kW at $20^\circ$C) and rises under heat ($\approx 1.18$~kW at $35^\circ$C); we do not inflate refrigeration to create drama. CCS taper sensitivity uses a conservative research envelope over pack-feasible SOC intervals, not OEM-measured curves.

\subsection{Heavy Volvo diagnosis}

Under the same operational-coverage portfolio ladder ($q{=}5$), Volvo FH saturates at operational coverage $0.67$ (E$=$F tie; sites $\{y_{\mathrm{settat\_ca}},y_{\mathrm{benguerir\_ca}}\}$); northbound Agadir$\rightarrow$Casablanca remains unserved in that panel (\texttt{results/final\_operational\_coverage\_vs\_budget\_volvo\_475.csv}). Do not transfer the eActros B2 $1.00$ ops claim to Volvo. Separately, selected Volvo configurations remain unserved under coarse $q{=}10$ while finer grids serve some ODs with empty site sets --- a discrete artifact, not a proof that Volvo cannot haul 15~t in Morocco.

\section{Interpretation for planners and researchers}
\label{sec:interpret}

\paragraph{Primary decisions.} Report minimum site counts and coverage under explicit scenario grades before any cost discussion.

\paragraph{Hypothetical infrastructure.} Benguerir stability on the northbound fine-grid panel supports \textbf{scenario-conditional} siting discussions only. Settat and other directions may dominate under different portfolios.

\paragraph{Numerical hygiene.} Treat coarse-grid infeasibility as a trigger for $q$-refinement and replay, not as a hard veto on electrification. When charge labels fall with $q$ but DRIVE arcs and installs are unchanged, attribute the shift to action-exact flooring (grade B+C), not to corridor choice.

\paragraph{Relation to Vidotte Plaza et al.\ \cite{VidottePlaza2026TRC}.} Their model integrates hours-of-service at network scale; our artifact complements that line with DEM-refrigerated Moroccan physics, chronological rest pads at verified stops, and an operational-coverage vs budget ladder that separates energy feasibility from executable schedules.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Joint siting and routing for electric heavy trucks is established prior art \cite{VidottePlaza2026TRC}. On this Moroccan refrigerated corridor pilot, the publishable core is \textbf{conditional operational coverage}: (i)~under a low-SOC three-mission mix, joint multi-route enumeration and greedy siting both reach coverage $1.00$ at two Benguerir directed sites and \textbf{tie}, while optimized fixed-route planning loses Casablanca$\rightarrow$Marrakech (E$-$C $= +1/3$ for eActros and Volvo); (ii)~under a six-OD depot-SOC panel, the same two Benguerir sites complete eActros coverage ($4/6\rightarrow 6/6$) with E$=$C$=$F, whereas Volvo retains a finer-grid $+1/6$ multi-route gap, including Marrakech$\rightarrow$Agadir service without new chargers; (iii)~northbound 15~t eActros feasibility is governed by departure SOC more than by adding a second AC site---80\% remains unserved, 100\% with one AC is operational. Matched feasible itineraries show no trip-time advantage. Energy-only coverage without schedule-valid rest is withdrawn as a primary claim. Universal optimality of Benguerir and nationwide freight claims are \textbf{not} supported; equal weights and hypothetical HGV CCS posts remain research proxies.

Future work: operator- and ADM-sourced OD, TRU, and charger measurements; richer northbound-weighted portfolios once observed counts exist.
