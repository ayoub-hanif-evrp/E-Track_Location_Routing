These are **small gains**: one additional mission represents 7.14 percentage points, and the gain is not consistent across vehicle and budget settings.

## **2. A new critical issue: the ablation results do not reproduce the headline results**

The repository has added a four-way comparison using the same charger sites under fixed-route and multi-route search. That is the right experimental design in principle.

But I re-aggregated the actual [ablation CSV](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/placement_flexibility_ablation.csv?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and obtained:

| Vehicle, two-site budget | Fixed on C sites | Multi on C sites | Fixed on E sites | Multi on E sites |
| ------------------------ | ---------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| eActros 600              | 8/14             | 8/14             | 9/14             | 9/14             |
| Volvo FH                 | 8/14             | 8/14             | 8/14             | 8/14             |

Yet the headline table reports **eActros C=10/14, E=10/14** and **Volvo C=8/14, E=9/14**.

This means the two tables are **not directly comparable as currently computed**. The likely reason is inconsistent search time limits: the headline pipeline retries selected `LIMIT_REACHED` missions with a larger budget, while the ablation uses the initial solver limits.

Within the ablation itself, **route flexibility adds zero served missions for either vehicle**. For eActros, the apparent one-mission difference comes from the **choice of charger locations**, specifically Marrakech → Casablanca, not from multi-route search.

That is a much narrower result than “joint routing improves coverage,” and the comparison must be regenerated with identical solver settings before making a causal claim.

## **3. Reliability and reproducibility remain major obstacles**

There are three distinct problems.

**First, unresolved solver runs can change the rankings.** The [solver certification table](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/solver_certification_table.csv?utm_source=chatgpt.com) reports:

| Two-site comparison | Confirmed served | `LIMIT_REACHED` missions |
| ------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| eActros C           | 10               | 1                        |
| eActros E           | 10               | 2                        |
| Volvo C             | 8                | 1                        |
| Volvo E             | 9                | 1                        |

These unresolved cases create uncertainty at least as large as the claimed one-mission gains. A time-limited search cannot establish that an unsolved mission is physically infeasible.

**Second, the green CI result needs qualification.** The latest run has **278 passed, 27 skipped, and 2 deselected**. Several graph-dependent tests now skip when the processed network CSVs are absent. This repairs the failing pipeline, but it does not restore the coverage those tests previously provided.

**Third, the published manifests do not match the checked-in files.** I compared GitHub’s tracked file sizes against both manifests:

- The main campaign manifest disagrees with the tracked byte sizes for **all 18 files it lists**.
- The release manifest references **two additional HOS CSVs that are absent** from the current tracked tree.
- The manifests identify the previous commit, `adc6287a`, rather than the current commit.

Many byte-size differences are exactly consistent with Windows CRLF-to-LF line-ending conversion, so this is not evidence that the numerical contents were deliberately changed. Nevertheless, a reader cannot verify the release using its advertised byte counts and hashes.

The relevant files are the [campaign manifest](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/campaign_manifest.json?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [release manifest](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/RELEASE/RELEASE_MANIFEST.json?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

## **4. The new driving-hours optimizer is real—but not complete**

The implementation is a meaningful technical improvement. The label-setting solver now carries a `drive_since_break_steps` resource, rejects excessive continuous-driving transitions, and permits rest actions at designated nodes. The new unit tests exercise that behavior.

However, two inconsistencies remain in the source code:

**The 9-hour daily limit is not enforced as a condition of serving a mission.** The [chronological HOS module](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/src/morocco_etruck/optimization/hos_adjustment.py?utm_source=chatgpt.com) calculates `daily_driving_ok`, but in [`evaluate_mission_operational`](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/scripts/run_final_operational_correction.py?utm_source=chatgpt.com), a successful continuous-driving replay can set `schedule_valid=True` without checking that flag. The search label itself also contains no daily-driving resource. Morocco’s decree 2-10-314, Article 17, sets a nine-hour daily driving limit.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://madda.ma\&sz=32)

madda.ma+1



**The search and replay disagree about qualifying breaks.** The [resource transition logic](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/src/morocco_etruck/optimization/resource_transitions.py?utm_source=chatgpt.com) resets the driving clock only when an individual CHARGE or WAIT action is long enough. The [schedule validator](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/src/morocco_etruck/optimization/operational_schedule.py?utm_source=chatgpt.com) combines consecutive CHARGE and WAIT actions into one break. For example, a 20-minute charge followed by a 25-minute wait can qualify in replay but does not reset the search clock. That can make the optimizer unnecessarily restrictive.

These findings do not prove that the 14 published missions violate the rules. They show that the implementation does **not yet establish full driving-hours compliance for general instances**. The legal break and split-break provisions also need to be represented consistently.&#x20;

## **5. Several “new campaign” outputs are still mixed with older evidence**

The current scripts and committed result files do not consistently represent the same experiment.

Most notably, the supposed [blind greedy benchmark](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/blind_greedy_vs_enum.csv?utm_source=chatgpt.com) still identifies its method as `greedy_over_exhaustive_enum_scores_vs_enum`. But the new Stage C code has a separate `blind_greedy_live_eval` procedure. Those are fundamentally different algorithms, and the checked-in CSV does not demonstrate the latter’s runtime or approximation quality.

The [larger-instance table](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/solver_larger_instance_table.csv?utm_source=chatgpt.com) likewise contains no benchmark larger than nine candidate sites. The current code explicitly defers such instances.

There is also a measurement issue: the HOS-constrained evaluator sets `energy_feasible=False` when the HOS search fails or times out. Therefore the new headline's “energy coverage” is **not an independent energy-only feasibility baseline**. This helps explain why the reported energy and operational coverage values are identical throughout the headline table. Those metrics must be separated with two controlled solves.

Finally, the payload–SOC frontier and robustness matrix have **not been comprehensively rerun under the new HOS pipeline**. The manuscript itself acknowledges the robustness evidence is legacy.

So the repository now has a stronger experimental framework, but it has **not yet produced one clean, internally consistent national experiment package**.

## **6. Numerical stability: some findings are useful, but fragile**

The newer [q=1 boundary study](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/results/paper_campaign/q1_boundary_refinement.csv?utm_source=chatgpt.com) provides valuable additional evidence.

For the eActros Agadir → Casablanca mission at 15 tonnes payload and 80% departure SOC, the single-Benguerir-charger scenario remains feasible at q=1. Its reported minimum SOC is approximately **10.45%**, against a 10% reserve.

That supports a narrower, scientifically interesting conclusion: **this mission is sensitive to energy discretization**, and the earlier assertion that it necessarily requires 100% departure SOC is incorrect.

But the remaining margin is only 0.45 percentage points of SOC. For an engineering claim involving uncertain speed, wind, road grade, refrigeration demand and charging acceptance, that is not a robust feasibility buffer.

Several other q=1 cases are recorded as `q1_status=done` with `q1_multi_ops=False`, despite very long computation times. The output does not preserve whether the solver proved infeasibility or simply exhausted its limits. Consequently, the q=1 table is not yet a complete convergence certificate.

This could become one of the paper's strongest contributions if framed around **numerical resolution and reliable feasibility classification**, rather than maximum coverage.

## **7. Novelty and manuscript quality**

The Moroccan setting, directional service-area constraints, DEM-based energy calculations, refrigerated loads and nonlinear charging model make this a potentially useful applied research contribution.

But the literature is competitive. A May 2026 *Transportation Research Part C* paper already studies charger location jointly with truck routing and driver working hours.&#x20;

The repository's contribution therefore cannot rest simply on integrating routing, charger location and HOS. It needs a result that existing methods would not already predict—for example, a quantitatively validated finding about directional service areas, refrigerated-truck charging feasibility, or how numerical approximations change infrastructure decisions.

The [manuscript](https://github.com/ayoub-hanif-evrp/E-Track_Location_Routing/blob/main/paper/manuscript.tex?utm_source=chatgpt.com) is more transparent than before, but remains closer to an extended research summary than a complete journal article. It needs a full mathematical formulation, a clear optimality/certification argument, detailed experimental design, uncertainty analysis, route-level case explanations, and a substantially deeper comparison with published methods.

The bibliography also contains an `Anonymous` author placeholder for a relevant paper, even though its authors and DOI are available. That should be fixed before submission.&#x20;

## **8. What must pass before submission**

For the high-impact optimization path you selected earlier, I would use the following acceptance gates, in order:

**Gate 1 — Correct and test HOS feasibility.** Add daily-driving limits, reconcile combined CHARGE+WAIT breaks, and test routes that violate daily but not continuous-driving rules. Every accepted itinerary must pass an independent physical-time audit.

**Gate 2 — Run a genuine HOS-aware placement campaign.** Re-enumerate candidate portfolios under the corrected solver rather than merely rescore sites selected before HOS was introduced. Use matched computational limits and resolve, or explicitly bound, all `LIMIT_REACHED` comparisons.

**Gate 3 — Establish causality.** At each budget, solve fixed and multi-route versions on identical C and E portfolios with the same solver settings. Reconcile every ablation total with the headline result. Report exactly which missions gain, which lose, and why.

**Gate 4 — Demonstrate numerical robustness.** Rerun disputed q=5/q=2/q=1 scenarios under the same HOS model, retain solver statuses, and assess whether reserve, weather and charging uncertainties reverse the decision.

**Gate 5 — Produce real algorithm benchmarks.** Run an independent greedy method, record number of evaluations and runtime, compare against exhaustive solutions on certifiable instances, and evaluate more than nine candidate sites to test scaling.

**Gate 6 — Freeze a reproducible release.** Ensure the experiment pipeline runs from a clean checkout, restore graph-dependent validation, regenerate hashes after Git normalization, include every referenced artifact, and bind all results to the exact release commit.

**Gate 7 — Strengthen external validity and the paper.** Validate assumptions against observed heavy-truck operations where possible, quantify the consequences of equal OD weights and normalized charger costs, and explain the mechanism behind every material improvement.

**My conclusion is that this repository has credible research potential, but its current results are not sufficiently certified to support a high-impact optimization paper.** The software has progressed faster than the evidence needed to substantiate the central claims.

The most defensible finding today is not that joint multi-route optimization substantially improves coverage. It is that **operational constraints, computational limits, charging-site directionality and energy-grid resolution materially affect which electric-truck missions appear feasible**. That can be a valuable scientific result—but it needs a controlled, reproducible experiment showing the mechanisms quantitatively.

For *Transportation Research Part C*, I would **not submit this version yet**. I would retain the high-impact path and require Gates 1–5 before interpreting the performance comparisons as final.
